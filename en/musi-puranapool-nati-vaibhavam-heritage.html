🏛️ Puranapool Heritage
Bridge of Four Centuries

Why is the past glory not possible now?

🌉 An Evening on Puranapool

Recently, I stood on Puranapool one evening. Below, plastic covers and animal carcasses were floating in dirty water, emitting foul smell. But history says that four centuries ago, the Qutb Shahis standing on the same bridge were mesmerized seeing the beauty of the river.

Puranapool gives us a rare opportunity to see two eras standing at one place. It is not just a bridge. It is a link between Hyderabad's history and the living Musi river. Those traces are now cracking.

🌊 Musi Lifeline - 240 KM - Built in 1578

Originating in the Ananthagiri hills and traveling 240 km to merge with Krishna at Wadapally, Musi is the lifeline of Hyderabad. Puranapool was constructed in 1578 during the Qutb Shahi period, with the idea of facilitating movement between Golconda Fort and the region beyond Musi.

Built with twenty-six stone arches, this bridge was described as the "Bridge of Love" by French traveler Tavernier. At that time, Musi near Puranapool was very wide. Boats used to ply in the river and people used to bathe.

👑 Foreign Travelers Description & 1908 Great Flood

Foreign travelers who visited Hyderabad in the 17th century described that the gardens and buildings on the banks of Musi river were captivating to the viewers.

But the same Musi showed devastation with heavy floods in 1908. With incessant rains, Musi took a ferocious form, touching Puranapool and inundating Afzal Gunj, Begum Bazar areas on the banks. Modern Hyderabad was born from that destruction - twin reservoirs Osman Sagar and Himayat Sagar were created with the foresight of the Nizam government.

🛰️ My Observation as a Geographer

As a geographer, I examined Puranapool with the help of satellite images. In the last three decades, the natural width of Musi river near Puranapool has reduced drastically. That means we have swallowed the river! The green places and open spaces that were once seen on both sides of the river are no longer visible.

Over time, constructions have come up in the buffer zone in the river bed.

🏭 Sewage Load - Debris Dam Effect

Most of the sewage generated in Hyderabad directly joins Musi without treatment. Nalas like Bahadurpura Nala, Kishanbagh Nala, Pathargatti Nala located upstream of Puranapool discharge lakhs of litres of waste water into Musi every day.

🔬 Research Finding: As soon as it rains, all the waste gets stuck to the bridge pillars and creates Debris Dam Effect. Due to this, backwater effect causes reverse flow. As a result, low-lying areas like Chaderghat, Moosarambagh get submerged even with small rain.

✅ Smart Science - My Research Solutions

1. 1:1000 Scale 3D Map: A 3D map of Musi river should be prepared with drones and satellites. Every meter should be accounted - where is encroachment, where does nala join.

2. IoT Sensors: Sensors that measure water quality 24 hours should be installed at key points like Puranapool, Chaderghat, Nagole.

3. AHP Priority Table: If funds are spent by deciding where to fix first through Analytic Hierarchy Process, every rupee will be properly utilized.

4. Heritage Interpretation Center: If light and sound show, boating, food courts are set up, local youth will get employment - this is the Sabarmati success formula.

🎯 Conclusion - Our Goal

What is seen when looking down from Puranapool is not just dirty water. Our negligence, our policy flaw, and the threat looming over our future are visible. Musi warned us in 1908. The floods in 2023, 2024 reminded us again.

It is our responsibility to show our children the beautiful Musi that the Qutb Shahis saw four centuries ago. Puranapool should stand proudly for another 400 years. Musi should flow fully and cleanly beneath it.
